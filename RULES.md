# Stock Ticker Rules

Buy and sell shares in six commodities while dice move the market. When the clock runs out, the player with the highest net worth wins. Net worth is cash plus the current value of every share you hold.

## The commodities

Every game has its own prices. All six stocks start at **$1.00** and move between $0.00 and $2.00.

| Stock      | Symbol | What it is                                      |
| ---------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Grain      | GRN    | Wheat, corn, and other farm crops               |
| Industrial | IND    | Manufacturers and heavy industry                |
| Bonds      | BNDS   | Government and corporate debt                   |
| Oil        | OIL    | Crude oil and fuel                              |
| Silver     | SLVR   | The cheaper precious metal, also used in industry |
| Gold       | GOLD   | The classic store of value                      |

The board always shows them in this order.

## Starting out

Each player starts with **$5,000** and no shares. This holds for players who join after the game has started, too.

## Taking a turn

Players take turns in the order they joined. On your turn:

1. **Roll the dice.** The market moves.
2. **Roll again.** The market moves a second time.
3. **Buy and sell.** Make as many trades as you like.
4. **Press Done Trading.** Play passes to the next player.

You cannot trade before both rolls, and you cannot end your turn early. Players who are waiting can watch every roll and trade as it happens.

## The dice

Each roll throws three dice. Every face on every die is equally likely.

| Die    | Decides                 | Faces                                       |
| ------ | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Stock  | Which stock moves       | Grain, Industrial, Bonds, Oil, Silver, Gold |
| Action | What happens to it      | Up, Down, Dividend                          |
| Amount | How much                | 5¢, 10¢, 20¢                                |

- **Up** raises the price by the amount. "Oil, Up, 10¢" takes Oil from $1.00 to $1.10.
- **Down** lowers the price by the amount.
- **Dividend** pays everyone who holds that stock. The price does not change.

## Special events

### Stock split

When a price reaches **$2.00**, the stock splits. Everyone holding it gets double the shares, and the price resets to $1.00. Any amount over $2.00 is lost: a stock at $1.90 that rolls Up 20¢ still resets to $1.00.

A split never changes what your shares are worth at that moment. Twice as many shares at half the price is the same money, and the stock can climb again from $1.00.

### Worthless stock

When a price falls to **$0.00**, the stock is worthless. Everyone holding it loses those shares for nothing, and the price resets to $1.00. Play continues as normal.

### Dividends

A dividend pays only when the stock is at **$1.00 or more**. Below $1.00 the roll has no effect.

The payout is the amount die read as a percentage of the current price, for every share you hold:

| Amount die | Pays per share |
| ---------- | -------------- |
| 5¢         | 5% of price    |
| 10¢        | 10% of price   |
| 20¢        | 20% of price   |

For example, holding 1,000 shares of Gold at $1.50 when Gold pays a 20% dividend earns 1,000 × $0.30 = $300.

## Buying and selling

- You trade only on your own turn, after both rolls.
- Shares trade in **lots of 500**. The quick buttons on each stock card buy or sell 1, 5, 10, or 25 lots, plus the most you can afford ("Max") or the most you own ("All").
- Trades happen at the current price.
- You need the cash for every purchase. You cannot borrow.
- You can sell only shares you own. There is no short selling.
- The supply of shares is unlimited. Anyone can buy as many as they can afford.

## The clock

When creating a game, the host picks 15, 30, 60, or 90 minutes. The clock starts when the host presses Start Game. It turns red with five minutes left and pulses in the last minute.

When the clock reaches zero, trading stops and the game ends. Players are ranked by net worth. Players with exactly the same net worth share a rank, and the one who joined earlier is listed first.

## Game types

### Solo

Create a game and start it without inviting anyone. A solo game is the only kind you can **pause**. Pausing stops the clock; resuming restarts it with the time you had left. If you leave a paused game, you can rejoin later from the lobby and resume.

### Multiplayer

Share the six-character invite code, or let players find your game in the lobby. There is no player limit. Players can join before or after the host starts the game. Late joiners take their turn after everyone who joined before them.

## Leaving and rejoining

You can leave at any time and come back with the same invite code. Your cash and shares stay as you left them, and splits, crashes, and dividends still apply to your shares while you are away.

If you leave during your own turn, the server makes any rolls you had left, then passes play to the next player. While you are away, your turns are skipped.

## Chat

Every game has a chat in the sidebar. Messages can be up to 200 characters, and emoji from your keyboard work.
